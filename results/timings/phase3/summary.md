## Cross-model comparison (end-to-end run time in seconds, medians)

Serial, OpenMP (8 threads) and MPI (8 ranks, one machine) read the binary sets; their times are from Phases 1 and 2. Hadoop reads the same images as base64 text from HDFS. Hadoop pipeline = Job 1 (statistics) + Job 2 (normalize), i.e. the same two passes. Uploading to HDFS is not included.

| input | Serial | OpenMP | MPI | Hadoop Job 1 | Hadoop Job 2 | Hadoop pipeline | Hadoop / Serial | map tasks (Job 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100MB (100 MiB) | 0.17 | 0.03 | 0.02 | 16.8 | 12.7 | 29.5 | 171x | 2 |
| 500MB (501 MiB) | 0.83 | 0.15 | 0.11 | 18.9 | 17.8 | 36.7 | 44x | 6 |
| full (6046 MiB) | 10.06 | 1.60 | 1.41 | 81.0 | 77.9 | 158.9 | 16x | 63 |

**When would Hadoop pay off?**

- Fixed cost per pipeline (two jobs: JVM start, YARN scheduling, container launch): about 27 s, measured as the intercept of a line through the 100MB and full runs.
- Marginal cost on this cluster: 21.8 ms per MiB of pixels, against 1.66 ms per MiB for the serial program. Hadoop's marginal cost is higher, so on this cluster it never catches up: more data only widens the gap.
- Hadoop used 135 s of CPU time for the full set (both jobs), 13 times the serial program's 10.1 s: decoding base64 text, JVM record handling and the streaming pipes cost more than the computation.
- With that CPU cost and ideal scaling, Hadoop needs about 13 busy cores to match the serial program and about 96 to match 8 MPI ranks, before counting the fixed 27 s. It wins when the data no longer fits one machine (disk, or a run time long enough that a node failure becomes likely), not on speed.
